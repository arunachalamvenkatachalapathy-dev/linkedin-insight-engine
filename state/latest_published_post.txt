We are trading chemical safety for systemic digital vulnerability in our race to scale battery recycling infrastructure. 

Industry leaders are rushing to deploy digital twins to manage the 40 percent higher risk of thermal runaway in recycling plants compared to standard e-waste facilities. 

These models provide vital real-time heat maps to predict electrolyte leakage before it happens. 

This creates a paradox. 

By tethering physical safety protocols to cloud-integrated predictive modeling, we are creating a dangerous architectural dependency. 

If a facility relies on these digital twins to manage thermodynamic baselines, the plant becomes vulnerable to sensor drift and firmware manipulation. 

Engineers must prioritize local logic redundancy. 

We need hard-coded safety overrides that function independently of the primary digital control system. 

Sustainability reporting is currently failing to address this intersection of climate tech and cybersecurity. 

We are digitizing our physical safety at a speed that our risk frameworks cannot match. 

If our safety protocols now rely on cloud-integrated digital twins, have we effectively traded physical chemical hazards for a catastrophic systemic digital vulnerability that ESG disclosures are ignoring?
